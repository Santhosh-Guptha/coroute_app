A ride with CoRoute

One Sunday, six riders, seven moments.

Meera, Imran, Kiran, Asha, Dev and Ravi ride as the Sunday Sunrise Crew. They are made up; everything the app does for them is real. Scroll along.

Skip the story

  1. Chapter 1 Friday, 9 pm

    Planning the Sunday ride

    Meera opens a poll in the crew: Sunday at 5:30 am, or Saturday? Five of six pick Sunday. She plans the route up to the ghat with a fuel stop and a breakfast stop, and the ride gets its join code.

    Before they leave, every phone runs the pre-ride check: location allowed all the time, battery, an emergency contact, and Nearby devices for SOS without signal.

  2. Chapter 2 Sunday, 5:40 am

    Rolling out together

    Six riders on one live map. Meera rides lead, Imran sweeps at the back, and everyone can see who is where without a phone call.

    The intercom is hands-free. Noise reduction takes the wind and the engine out, so "fuel in 10 km" is heard the first time. Voice goes live to the group and nothing is recorded.

  3. Chapter 3 6:50 am, on the ghat

    Bends, limits and a hazard ahead

    The road climbs in hairpins. Before each tight bend Asha hears it coming: "Hairpin right, comfortable 25". The speed limit stays in view; where no limit is posted, the estimate is marked est. and is never spoken.

    A rider earlier reported gravel two bends up, so the warning comes before the corner, not in it.

  4. Chapter 4 7:30 am, in the forest

    The signal drops. The group still talks.

    Under the trees the bars go to zero. The offline link takes over: the group's phones pass messages along, a few hundred metres per hop.

    Imran's "Fuel stop after the bridge" reaches Meera at the front through Asha, Dev and Kiran. The closer the group rides, the better it works. As soon as one phone finds signal, it sends the group's updates on.

  5. Chapter 5 8:10 am

    A rider falls behind

    Kiran stops for a photo and the gap grows. At about a kilometre the sweep and the lead are told, quietly, and the app suggests a regroup point: the fuel stop ahead.

    Everyone meets there. Nobody is quietly dropped.

  6. Chapter 6 10:05 am, after breakfast

    The crash

    On a wet bend Ravi slides off the road into the trees, out of sight of the others. His phone notices the hard fall and asks "Are you OK?" for 30 seconds. No answer.

    The SOS goes out: an SMS from his phone to his family contact, which needs no mobile data; an alert to the group, through the server and the offline link; and a live link his family can follow. The rescue beacon starts a siren that gets louder.

    Find Ravi

    The crew turns back. GPS gets them close. In the last stretch, where a map pin is not enough, "Find Ravi" guides them with a hotter or colder meter from his phone's Bluetooth signal and the locator sound. They reach him, call 112 and stay with him.

    CoRoute adds to emergency services; it does not replace them. Beacon ranges are short and depend on the place: the siren carries tens of metres, Bluetooth roughly 30 to 100 m.

  7. Chapter 7 Evening

    Everyone home

    Ravi is fine: a sprained wrist and a scratched tank. By evening the whole crew is home. The trip report shows the day: the distance, the moving time, the stops and the speed against the group limit. A ride summary arrives by e-mail.

    And Ravi's family had followed the live link all day, in a browser, with no app.

Why riders choose CoRoute

Six things CoRoute does on every ride.

Built for Indian highways, ghats and high passes, where a group spreads out, the signal comes and goes and help can be far away. Every detail: all features and how safety works.

  • Crash detection with a 30-second check

    A hard fall starts a loud alarm and a 30-second countdown, even over the lock screen. Tap I'm OK and nobody is disturbed. If you do not answer, your group and emergency contacts are alerted with your location.

  • SOS that works without signal

    No mobile data? Your phone texts your location by SMS. No network at all? The group's phones pass the SOS along, phone to phone over Bluetooth or a shared hotspot, a few hundred metres per hop, until one gets through. Once it reaches us, our server also texts your emergency contacts.

  • Hears sirens when your earbuds don't

    Noise-cancelling earbuds, a helmet set or music can hide an ambulance. During a ride CoRoute listens for ambulance, police and fire sirens on the phone and tells you, in your earbuds, to give way.

  • Live intercom, hands-free, clean voice

    Talk to the whole group or one rider, push-to-talk or hands-free, through your helmet headset or the phone. Voice cleanup takes out wind and breath. No extra hardware, and nothing is recorded.

  • Family follows live, no app needed

    Guardians open a private link in any browser. They see the level you choose, hear about an SOS or a long unexpected stop, and get a message when you arrive. The link ends with the ride.

  • Your data is encrypted, never sold or shared

    Everything travels encrypted, passwords are stored as one-way hashes, and your location goes only to your group during a ride. No ads, no trackers, no data sale. How we protect it

Your data is protected

Your location is for your group. Nobody else.

A touring app knows where you are for days at a time. We treat that seriously, and we say plainly who sees what.

  • Only during a ride. Live location is collected while you are in an active ride and shared only with that group.
  • Links that expire. Guardian links can be paused or revoked, and emergency links stop after 30 minutes or when the alert closes.
  • Voice is never recorded. Intercom voice is relayed live to your group and goes out only while you talk. Siren listening happens on your phone and nothing from it is sent.
  • Encrypted, never sold. Everything travels over HTTPS, passwords are stored as one-way hashes, and there are no ads, no trackers and no sale of data.
  • Yours to delete. Recorded routes are deleted after 90 days, and you can delete your account at any time in the app or on the account deletion page.
