Coroute Privacy Policy

Effective 8 October 2026 · Coroute is a free, non-profit app for group riders, operated by devmonks.space.

Privacy Policy Terms of Use

Summary

Privacy, in plain words.

  • Coroute is free and non-profit. We do not sell data, we show no ads, and we have no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • Your live location is collected only while you are in an active convoy. It is shown to the members of that convoy, and sharing stops when you leave, the convoy ends or you sign out.
  • Voice is relayed live and never stored. We keep only who talked to whom and for how long, for 7 days, to detect abuse.
  • Crash detection runs on your phone. Motion data is not sent to us and not stored, and you can switch crash detection off.
  • In an SOS or crash alert your own group is always alerted first. A few riders of other groups on the same road may be asked to help, at most 3 at a time. They never get your phone number, and you can turn this off.
  • Medical info is optional. Your convoy sees it only while your SOS or crash alert is open, and administrators never see it.
  • Emergency texts and Groups nearby are off until they are switched on.
  • Recorded routes and exact positions are deleted after 90 days. You can delete your account in the app at any time, and it is removed at once.

This is a short summary. The full text below has the details.

Coroute helps a group of riders stay together: it shows every member of your convoy on a map, lets you send quick status messages and SOS alerts, and provides a live voice intercom. This policy explains exactly what the app collects, why, how long it is kept, and how to delete it. We do not sell data, we show no ads, and we have no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. Our Terms of Use also apply.

Location, including in the background

While you are in an active convoy, Coroute shares your position with that convoy even when the screen is off, so your ride mates can still see you and your SOS still works. A persistent notification is shown on Android while this is happening. Location sharing stops automatically when you leave the convoy, when the convoy ends, or when you sign out. No location is collected when you are not in a convoy. If your phone has no signal, the points are kept on your phone and sent when the connection returns.

If the theme is set to Automatic, the app uses the last position your phone already knows to work out sunrise and sunset. This happens on your phone only; that position is not sent to us.

Crash detection and the motion sensor

During a ride, above 25 km/h, the app can read the phone's motion sensor to notice a crash. This happens on your phone only. Motion data is not sent to us and not stored. If the phone thinks you crashed, it asks "Possible accident detected. Are you okay?" and counts down for 15 seconds first. If you tap "I'm OK", nothing is sent. If you tap "Need Help" or do not answer, an SOS marked as automatic goes to your convoy with your position, direction, speed before the impact and the time. You can switch crash detection off in the app.

Our server also notices when a rider stops suddenly from high speed and does not move for 2 minutes, or loses signal right after riding fast. It then asks the lead and the nearest riders to check on that rider. This uses only the position and speed your convoy already sees.

Help from nearby riders of other groups

When you raise an SOS or a crash alert, your own group is always alerted first. Our server also looks for a few riders of other groups who are already riding toward your position on the same road and may reach you sooner than your own group. At most 3 riders are asked at a time, never everyone nearby. Before they agree to help they see only the place of the emergency, how far it is and roughly how long it takes to get there. They do not see your name, your group, your phone number, your ride mates or anyone's position other than the emergency point.

If one of them taps "I Can Help", that rider also sees your first name and your vehicle type and colour, so they can find you. Your medical info is shown to them only if you switched on "Share medical info with a rider from another group who comes to help me" (off unless you turn it on). Phone numbers are never given to riders of other groups; they can call emergency services. Your group sees that "a nearby rider" is coming, their first name, how far away they are and their position while on the way, but not their group. When the emergency is closed, the riders of other groups lose access to it at once and their phones drop what they were shown.

Riders of other groups who are riding toward an accident on the same road get a short warning ("Rider accident reported 2 km ahead") with the place only, no names.

You can turn off "Help nearby riders" (you are then never asked) and "Ask nearby riders to help me" (other groups are then never asked for you; your own group still is) in the app at any time. This works the same whether your group is Public or Private.

Groups nearby (optional)

This is off unless the lead of your group sets the group to Public and turns on "Nearby group discovery". Only when two groups have both done this, each group sees the other group's name, how many riders it has, a rounded distance and whether it is riding the same way or toward you, for example "Weekend Riders nearby, 6 riders, about 4.5 km". Never member names, positions, phone numbers or destination. A group can send a one-tap wave; there is no chat. A group with an open emergency is never shown.

Emergency texts when there is no internet

This is off until you switch it on. When it is on and your SOS cannot reach the server, your own phone sends a text message (SMS) with a map link to your position. It goes to your emergency contact, your lead and the nearest riders in your convoy, at most 10 people. The texts are sent from your phone and your SIM, not by us, and your carrier may charge for them.

To make this work, your phone keeps the phone numbers of the other riders in your convoy during the ride. It gets only their numbers and their role in the ride, not their names. The numbers are stored encrypted on the phone, are never shown on screen, and are deleted when the ride ends, when you leave or sign out, or when you switch the setting off. They are used for nothing else.

If you do not want to receive these texts, switch off "Receive emergency texts from my ride group" in your profile. Your number is then not given to other riders for texts. Your ride mates can still see your number on your rider card, as before.

Spoken alerts and the ride notification

The app can read out emergency alerts and warnings using your phone's own text-to-speech engine. The text is spoken on your phone and is not sent anywhere. During a ride the notification shows your destination, the nearest riders ahead and behind with their distance, and quick actions. On the lock screen it shows your own ride only if "Show ride on lock screen" is on; emergencies of other groups show only a short line without names, and phone numbers are never shown there. You can switch spoken alerts, the lock screen view and the large notification off in the app.

Microphone

The microphone is used only while you hold the Talk button or have armed the hands-free (VOX) mode, and only inside an active convoy. Your voice is sent live to the members of your convoy (or to the single rider you selected) and is not recorded or stored by us.

Who can see your data

Members of the convoy you are in see your name, vehicle, phone, emergency contact, live position and messages. If you filled in medical info, they see it only while your SOS or crash alert is open. Medical info is never shown to administrators, never written into the ride timeline and never put in notifications. They can also see your recorded route, stops and timeline entries, but only for the time they were in that convoy themselves. Nobody outside your convoy can see them. Service administrators can see active convoys for safety operations (for example to dissolve an abusive convoy or send a safety announcement), open SOS alerts with who raised them, where and who is going to help, the safety log and false alarm counts; they cannot see medical info or listen to intercom audio. Riders of other groups see only what is described under "Help from nearby riders of other groups" and "Groups nearby".

Where your data lives

Data is stored in an Oracle Cloud Autonomous Database and relayed by our server, both in the Oracle Cloud Hyderabad region (India). Connections use TLS. Passwords are stored as bcrypt hashes. If you sign in with Google, we receive your e-mail and display name from Google; Google's own privacy policy governs that sign-in.

Your choices and rights

You can edit your profile and delete individual trips inside the app. You can delete your account in the app under Account & security, Delete my account; it is removed at once. This deletes your profile, your trips, your recorded routes, your timeline entries, your safety log rows and false alarm count, and your chat messages, SOS alerts and voice log entries. The rides you shared stay in the other riders' history, but your name there is replaced with "Former rider", and your phone, bike number and emergency contact are removed. If you can no longer sign in, e-mail santhoshbukka5@gmail.com from the address you registered with and we delete it within 30 days. You may also request a copy of your data. You can revoke location and microphone permissions at any time in your phone's settings; the app will then work in a limited mode. You can switch crash detection, emergency texts, help from nearby riders, spoken alerts and the lock screen view off at any time in the app.

Children

Coroute is intended for licensed riders aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from children.
