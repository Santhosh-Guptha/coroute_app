Summary

Privacy, in plain words.

  • We do not sell data, we show no ads, and we have no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. Group plans are bought on our website, not in the app, and hosting group rides may be free for everyone at times.
  • Your live location is collected only while you are in an active convoy. It is shown to the members of that convoy, and sharing stops when you leave, the convoy ends or you sign out.
  • Voice is relayed live and never stored. We keep only who talked to whom and for how long, for 7 days, to detect abuse.
  • Crash detection runs on your phone and is always on during a ride. Motion data is not sent to us and not stored.
  • In an SOS or crash alert your own group is always alerted first. A few riders of other groups on the same road may be asked to help, at most 3 at a time. This is always on, and they never get your phone number.
  • Medical info is optional. If you fill it in, it is shown on your lock screen and to the people helping you (your convoy, your guardians and a rider who accepted to help), only while your SOS or crash alert is open. Administrators never see it.
  • Emergency texts are always on: when you raise an SOS, or a crash alarm runs out without an answer, your phone texts your emergency contact from your own number (if you allowed CoRoute to send texts), with your position and a live link. If your SOS cannot reach our server, your ride mates get a text too. Groups nearby stays off until your group lead turns it on.
  • If you join a club or a tour operator's ride, the organiser sees your username and first name, your live position only during their rides, and your emergency contact only if you raise an SOS during their ride.
  • During an SOS you, or your lead, can share a live link that shows your first name, last position and update time for 30 minutes. Weather and nearest hospital lookups send only rounded route points or the alert position through our server. Saved map tiles stay on your phone.
  • Ride Guardian links offer PIN-protected web monitoring for family across 3 privacy tiers (Basic, Live, Emergency). Session and capability tokens are hashed, grants can be paused or revoked at any time by the rider, and access expires automatically.
  • Route Essentials searches for fuel, puncture repair, food, and medical within 1.5 km of the road via OpenStreetMap Overpass without sending rider identities or tracking profiles; responses are cached on the server.
  • Smart Fuel Profile keeps tank capacity, mileage, and reserve buffers stored locally on your device; raw tank inputs are never uploaded, and only opted-in usable range estimates are shared.
  • Quick dialing for 108 Ambulance, 112 National Emergency, and 100 Police connects through the device native dialer directly to public emergency operators; CoRoute does not record or route emergency calls.
  • Sweeper assignments and rider-down incident reports are shared only with the active group (and approaching riders for road safety warnings), with no private personal data leaked.
  • The app keeps a technical log on your phone to help fix problems. It has no messages, names, phone numbers or exact places, and it is sent to us only when you choose: from Profile, Help, Send feedback, or when you say yes to the question after a ride with an alert.
  • We send a welcome e-mail when you join, receipts, and a short summary of your own rides. Ride summaries and tips and news can be turned off in your website account or with the link in each e-mail. Offer e-mails are sent only if you turn them on. You can turn off each kind of news notification in the app; safety notifications always arrive.
  • Recorded routes and exact positions are deleted after 90 days. You can delete your account in the app (Profile, Account, Delete account) and it is removed at once. A request through our deletion page is completed within 48 hours. Backup copies expire within 30 days.

CoRoute helps a group of riders stay together: it shows every member of your convoy on a map, lets you send quick status messages and SOS alerts, and provides a live voice intercom. This policy explains exactly what the app collects, why, how long it is kept, and how to delete it. Group plans are bought on our website; hosting group rides may also be free for everyone at times. We do not sell data, we show no ads, and we have no third-party analytics or tracking SDKs. Our Terms of Use also apply.

Location, including in the background

While you are in an active convoy, CoRoute shares your position with that convoy even when the screen is off, so your ride mates can still see you and your SOS still works. A persistent notification is shown on Android while this is happening. Location sharing stops automatically when you leave the convoy, when the convoy ends, or when you sign out. No location is collected when you are not in a convoy. If your phone has no signal, the points are stored in a local encrypted buffer on your phone and synced when the connection returns.

Recorded route points are retained securely for 90 days to enable personal ride replay and statistics in your trip history, after which raw coordinate traces are permanently purged from the database.

If the theme is set to Automatic, the app uses the last position your phone already knows to work out sunrise and sunset. This happens on your phone only; that position is not sent to us.

Crash detection and the motion sensor

During a ride, from about 12 km/h, the app reads the phone's motion sensor to notice a crash. This happens on your phone only. Motion data is not sent to us and not stored. If the phone thinks you crashed, it asks "Possible accident detected. Are you okay?" and counts down for 30 seconds first. If you tap "I'm OK", nothing is sent. If you tap "Need Help" or do not answer, an SOS marked as automatic goes to your convoy with your position, direction, speed before the impact and the time. Crash detection is part of CoRoute's safety features and is always on during a ride (since November 2026; earlier versions of the app had a switch).

Our server also notices when a rider stops suddenly from high speed and does not move for 2 minutes, or loses signal right after riding fast. It then asks the lead and the nearest riders to check on that rider. This uses only the position and speed your convoy already sees.

Help from nearby riders of other groups

When you raise an SOS or a crash alert, your own group is always alerted first. Our server also looks for a few riders of other groups who are already riding toward your position on the same road and may reach you sooner than your own group. At most 3 riders are asked at a time, never everyone nearby. Before they agree to help they see only the place of the emergency, how far it is and roughly how long it takes to get there. They do not see your name, your group, your phone number, your ride mates or anyone's position other than the emergency point.

If one of them taps "I Can Help", that rider also sees your first name and your vehicle type and colour, so they can find you. While your alert is open they also see the medical info you entered (blood group, allergies, notes) and your emergency contact, so they can tell a doctor and reach your family; there is no switch for this, and it is removed when the alert closes. Your own phone number and your ride mates' numbers are never given to riders of other groups; they can call emergency services. Your group sees that "a nearby rider" is coming, their first name, how far away they are and their position while on the way, but not their group. When the emergency is closed, the riders of other groups lose access to it at once and their phones drop what they were shown.

Riders of other groups who are riding toward an accident on the same road get a short warning ("Rider accident reported 2 km ahead") with the place only, no names.

"Help nearby riders" and "Ask nearby riders to help me" are part of CoRoute's safety features and are always on (since November 2026; earlier versions of the app had a switch for each). What riders of other groups see stays as described above: before anyone accepts, only the emergency point and how far it is; after one accepts, the details listed above, only while your alert is open. Your own group is always told as well. This works the same whether your group is Public or Private. Nearby help can be paused for everyone by CoRoute (for example during maintenance); your own group is still told.

Groups nearby (optional)

This is off unless the lead of your group sets the group to Public and turns on "Nearby group discovery". Only when two groups have both done this, each group sees the other group's name, how many riders it has, a rounded distance and whether it is riding the same way or toward you, for example "Weekend Riders nearby, 6 riders, about 4.5 km". Never member names, positions, phone numbers or destination. A group can send a one-tap wave; there is no chat. A group with an open emergency is never shown.

Emergency texts from your phone

This is always on (since November 2026; earlier versions of the app had a switch). When you raise an SOS, or a crash alarm counts down without an answer (also a "Bike down?" check or a missed check-in that raises an SOS), your phone sends a text message (SMS) to your emergency contact by itself, even with the screen locked: your first name, what happened, the time, a live link and your position. When the SOS is closed, the same people get a short "safe now" text.

These texts are sent from your own phone number, through your own mobile carrier, not by CoRoute. Your carrier's normal SMS charges apply. The app asks for the Android permission to send texts only after telling you what it is for; it cannot read your messages. If you do not allow it, the messages app opens with the text ready and you, or someone with you, press Send. To limit costs the app sends at most 30 emergency texts a day. It keeps the numbers it still has to text only on your phone and deletes them 48 hours after the text was sent.

When your SOS cannot reach our server, your lead and sweeper and the nearest riders in your convoy (at most 10 people) get the text too.

CoRoute's servers do not send these texts. Our server can send them through an SMS provider (MSG91 or Twilio) only if CoRoute turns that on; it is off today. If it is turned on, the provider receives the contact's phone number, your first name, the live link and the time; nothing else.

To make this work, your phone keeps the phone numbers of the other riders in your convoy during the ride. It gets only their numbers and their role in the ride, not their names. The numbers are stored encrypted on the phone, are never shown on screen, and are deleted when the ride ends, or when you leave or sign out. They are used for nothing else.

If you do not want to receive these texts, switch off "Receive emergency texts from my ride group" in your profile. Your number is then not given to other riders for texts. Your ride mates can still see your number on your rider card, as before.

Spoken alerts and the ride notification

The app can read out alerts and warnings using your phone's own text-to-speech engine. The text is spoken on your phone and is not sent anywhere. You can switch voice alerts off in Settings; emergencies are always spoken. During a ride the notification shows your destination, the nearest riders ahead and behind with their distance, and quick actions. Your ride, with the distances and an SOS button, is always shown on the lock screen during a ride, so the SOS button can be reached without unlocking. Emergencies of other groups show only a short line without names, and phone numbers are never shown there.

Microphone and Voice Intercom

Voice intercom: the microphone sends voice only during an active convoy, and sending is OFF by default. Voice goes out only while you physically hold the Push-to-Talk (PTT) button or when you have explicitly armed Voice-Operated Transmission (VOX) mode. Audio streams are encrypted and relayed directly to convoy members in real time over secure WebSockets. We never record, analyze, transcribe, or store audio content. Audio buffers are discarded immediately upon transmission.

Siren alerts: during a ride, and only while the Siren alerts setting is on (it is on by default), the app listens through the phone's built-in microphone for the sound pattern of ambulance, police and fire engine sirens, so it can warn riders who cannot hear outside. This listening happens entirely on your phone: the audio is analysed in short pieces and thrown away, and nothing from it is recorded or sent to us or anyone else. The microphone is released when the ride ends, during phone calls, when the battery is low, or when you switch Siren alerts off in Settings.

Live emergency links

While your SOS or crash alert is open, you or the lead of your group can create a live link and send it to anyone, for example a family member or the emergency services. Nobody else can create one. The link opens a public web page on our site that shows your first name, your last position, the time of that position, the time the link stops working, and a "Call 112" button. Nothing else: no phone number, no group, no other riders, no medical info.

A link works for 30 minutes. It stops earlier when the rider or the lead revokes it in the app, or when the alert is closed. After that the page says only that the link has expired. The link is a random code that we store only as a hash, so a lost link cannot be recovered or reopened. At most 3 links can be made for one alert.

Anyone with the link can open the page. Requests from one network address are rate limited, and the addresses are not stored beyond the normal server logs described above. The page sets no cookies and does not count page views. The map on the page is drawn from OpenStreetMap map images, which the viewer's browser loads from openstreetmap.org. The safety log keeps who created or revoked a link, and that a link was viewed, with the time and the alert id only; never the link itself and never a position.

Ride Guardian links

Ride Guardian links provide PIN-protected web monitoring for family and trusted contacts to follow your ride without needing a CoRoute account. You choose one of three privacy tiers for each link:

  • Basic: aggregate status and progress without exact coordinates.
  • Live: personal location during active ride only.
  • Emergency: location only when SOS or crash alert is open.

Session tokens and capability tokens are stored only as cryptographic hashes on our servers, ensuring raw credentials cannot be extracted from database storage. Observers cannot see other convoy members, group chat or emergency contacts. While the rider's SOS or crash alert is open they see the medical info the rider entered (blood group, allergies, notes). Grants can be paused or revoked at any time by the rider in the app. Access expires automatically when the ride ends or the chosen expiry duration lapses. All dependent records are purged upon convoy deletion or account removal.

Weather on the route

When you review a planned route and once more when the ride starts, the app can ask for a weather line such as "Rain likely after Warangal around 3 PM". To do this it sends up to 5 points along your planned route, rounded to about 10 km, to MET Norway (the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, api.met.no) through our server. Our server does not send your name, your account or your exact position, and it keeps the answer for at least 30 minutes so that other riders at the same places get the same answer without a new request. Nothing is sent when data saver is on. Weather: based on data from MET Norway, licence CC BY 4.0 / NLOD 2.0; we turn their hourly forecast into the short rain line you see. If a server is set to use Open-Meteo instead, the same rounded points go to open-meteo.com and the credit reads "Weather data by Open-Meteo.com".

Nearest hospital

When an SOS or crash alert opens, our server asks OpenStreetMap (Nominatim, the same service we already use for place names) for the nearest hospital to the alert position, once per alert. The result, for example "Nearest hospital: X, 4.2 km", is shown to your group with the alert. Only the position of the alert, rounded to about 1 km, is sent; no names or account details. The answer is kept for 30 days so that a new alert within about 1 km needs no new request. The alert itself never waits for this lookup.

Maps saved on your phone

When you plan or start a ride, the app downloads the map tiles along your planned route (and around your stops and your group) from OpenStreetMap and keeps them on your phone, so the map still shows when there is no signal. This is automatic. The saved map of a trip is deleted 7 days after the trip ends, and all saved maps together are kept under 300 MB (the least recently used go first). Maps of past trips are shown from the internet and are not downloaded. Nothing about this leaves your phone, and we do not know which tiles were saved.

Route Essentials

Route Essentials helps you find critical services along your planned path. Corridor queries search for fuel, puncture repair, food, and medical within 1.5 km of the road.

CoRoute queries OpenStreetMap Overpass without sending rider identities or tracking profiles; queries use only the corridor coordinates around the route. Query responses are cached on the server so riders traveling the same route do not trigger duplicate external requests.

Smart Fuel Profile

Smart Fuel Profile calculates estimated motorcycle range to help convoys plan collective fuel stops. Tank capacity, mileage, and reserve buffers remain stored locally on your device.

Only opted-in, fresh usable range estimates (in km) are shared with your ride group for collective fuel stop planning. Raw tank inputs are never uploaded. You can disable fuel range sharing in your settings at any time.

Sweeper, battery and update gaps

Your battery level and the time of your last position update are already shared with your ride group as part of your live location. Since 3.16 the app also uses them for alerts: the lead can make one rider the sweeper, and the lead and the sweeper are told when a rider's battery is low, when a rider has not sent an update for unusually long, or when a rider drops behind the sweeper. This adds no new data; it only shows what the group already sees in a clearer way.

Sweeper assignments and rider-down incident reports are shared only with the active group (and approaching riders for road safety warnings), with no private personal data leaked. When a rider-down report is filed, approaching riders of other groups on the same route receive only an anonymized road hazard alert and distance warning, without rider names, phone numbers, or group identities.

Indian emergency services

In emergency and safety screens, the app provides quick dialing shortcuts for Indian emergency services: 108 Ambulance, 112 National Emergency, and 100 Police.

Quick dialing for 108 Ambulance, 112 National Emergency, and 100 Police connects through the device native dialer directly to public emergency operators. CoRoute does not record or route emergency calls.

Hard braking count

During a ride the phone counts hard stops from the motion sensor that crash detection already reads. The count is shown to you on your own post-ride summary and your group never sees it. For your rider score, the app sends five numbers per finished ride: hard brakes, breaks, the highest tiredness level, break reminders and minutes over the speed limit. Never your route, position, times or speed. This is always on (since November 2026); rides you finished while an earlier version had it switched off are never sent. Your score is shown only to you; the public leaderboards follow the rules below.

Road safety warnings and "Help make roads safer"

Warnings for everyone. Every rider can get gentle warnings about hazard spots, rough road, twisty stretches and long gaps without fuel, a break reminder, and a pre-ride tip for busy hours in a region. These come from anonymous totals of many riders; using them sends nothing about you beyond the map area your phone downloads (rounded to areas of about 40 by 20 km).

Sharing is your choice (off by default). If you turn on "Help make roads safer" (app Settings, or your account on this website), CoRoute uses these signals from your rides to build those totals: hard braking, sudden swerves and skids, potholes and rough road (each as a cell of about 150 m, its direction, a speed band and the hour), the SOS and crash alerts you raise (cell only), per-ride road counters in cells of about 1 km (distance, moving time, how much the road turns, stops of 4 to 90 minutes), and, for crash alarms, the sensor numbers and how the alarm ended (no position), so that future alarms raise fewer false alarms. We record the time you turned it on or off.

What we never use. Your full route, your name, rider id, phone number or medical details. The first and last 300 m of every ride are left out, and nothing near saved home or work places. Instead of your id, stored rows carry a one-way code that changes every month and cannot be turned back into your id; it is used only to count how many different riders passed a spot. A spot is shown only once at least 5 different riders contributed to it.

How long. The raw signals are deleted after 90 days. The totals fade over time (a half-life of 90 days) and hold no link to any rider. Crash alarm training rows keep only sensor numbers, the phone maker, the month and the outcome.

Your controls. Turn sharing off at any time; collection stops at once. "Download my contributions" and "Delete my contributions" are in the app Settings and on your account page. Deleting removes everything from the last 90 days, including its share of the totals; older contributions exist only inside anonymous totals that cannot be traced back to you, so they cannot be split out. Deleting your account does the same. Weather is not used for these warnings.

Medical ID on the lock screen

This is always on. Only while your own SOS or crash alert is open, the ride notification on your lock screen shows your blood group, allergies and emergency contact name and number, so that a person who finds you can read them without unlocking the phone. This is shown by your phone only and is not sent anywhere beyond what the section "Who can see your data" already describes.

Bluetooth and Offline Mesh Relay

CoRoute utilizes Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) strictly for offline peer-to-peer convoy coordination and emergency failover. Outside of active offline convoy mesh relay, CoRoute does not use Bluetooth. Bluetooth is never used for background tracking, advertising identifiers, or third-party beacons. BLE scanning and broadcasting operate only while an active convoy is in progress and terminate automatically when the ride ends.

Trip reviews and group photos

After a ride the app can ask for a rating, a few words and up to three photos of your group. Photos are only sent if you tick the box that lets CoRoute publish your photo and story after review. The app makes each photo smaller before sending it, and the server removes location and camera details (EXIF and similar data) before storing it. Photos are kept in a private folder on the CoRoute server, not on the public website.

The CoRoute team reads every review and looks at every photo. Only what an admin approves, and only the photos an admin picks, appear on the website, with a "Rider #" label instead of your name. Your e-mail address and phone number are never shown. Editing your review takes it off the website until it is checked again. You can withdraw it at any time from the trip report; it and its photos leave the website at once.

Profile picture

A profile picture is optional. When you add one, the app or the website lets you move, zoom and turn it under a round frame. Two files are then stored: the round picture other signed-in riders see (512 pixels at most), and the picture you chose, made smaller (2,048 pixels at most), so that you can adjust the round picture later without choosing it again. Only you can open that original; other riders, guardians and the public website never get it. Both files are written again before they are sent, and the server removes location and camera details (EXIF and similar data) once more, keeping only which way up the picture is. Removing your picture, an admin removing it for breaking the rules, and deleting your account each delete both files.

Numbers on our website

The home page can show a few all-time totals, such as riders (registered riders with at least one completed ride), hours on the road, rides completed and kilometres ridden, and how many riders are on the road right now. They are worked out on our server from completed rides and are totals only: never per city, per route, per ride or per rider. Before they leave the server, counts are rounded down to tens (hundreds from 1,000), distance to 100 km (1,000 km from 10,000 km), and anything below 10 shows as nothing, so a small number can not point at you. An average rating is only shown once at least 10 ratings are behind it, and times are given to the hour at most.

The home, safety and features pages can also show "CoRoute impact" totals: kilometres and hours ridden, rides and group rides, the size of the largest group and the length of the longest trip (as a number only, with no name, route, place or date), bikes on CoRoute, and what the safety features did, such as SOS and crash alerts, alerts answered by other riders, the typical response time and road hazards reported. These are coarse aggregates under the same rules: rounded down, nothing below 10, the largest group only once at least 10 group rides exist, the longest trip rounded down to 10 km and only once at least 10 rides exist, and the response time only from at least 10 answered alerts. They never describe a rider, a ride or a place.

The public pages of the website set no cookies and load no trackers or third-party scripts. They count visits per page and day (with the name of the referring site), never per person. Your own ride analytics in the app are worked out from your own rides and are shown only to you.

When you sign in on the website (your plan, payments and receipts), one strictly necessary cookie keeps you signed in. It can only be read by our server (not by scripts on the page), works only over HTTPS on this site, and ends when you sign out, after 60 minutes without activity, and after 7 days at most. It is never used for analytics or advertising.

How we protect your account and data: phone numbers, your emergency contact and your Medical ID details are stored encrypted on our servers (AES-256), and passwords only as salted one-way hashes. Each phone or browser you sign in with is a session you can see and end under "Where you're signed in" on your account page or with "Sign out of all devices" (also in the app). Changing your password ends every other session. We e-mail you when your account signs in on a new device. In the app, sign-in details are kept in your phone's protected keystore and are not included in phone backups.

Signing in on another phone during a ride: while your phone is on a ride (or has an SOS open), a sign-in on another device is refused unless you use a one-time code we e-mail to you. To recognise your own phone (for example after a reinstall), the app sends a one-way hash of an app-specific Android device ID and a random installation ID with each sign-in; our server keeps only a further keyed hash of them, uses them for nothing else, and deletes them with the session record. New accounts confirm their e-mail address with a link before they can be used; accounts never confirmed are deleted after 7 days. The app can optionally be locked with your fingerprint or screen lock; that check happens on your phone and nothing about it is sent.

Public leaderboards

The website's leaderboards (the home page and the leaderboard page) list the top riders by rider score, longest ride, kilometres, hours and rides. A rider appears only by the unique username chosen in the app, next to rounded totals. Never your real name, e-mail, phone number, bike, photo, routes, places, or the dates and times of your rides. Riders can opt out: hide your name from the leaderboards in the app and your username is left off every board.

Understanding how CoRoute is used

Once a day we count things like how many riders signed up, rode and bought a plan, and which riders have not ridden for a while. We use ride dates, plan dates and the hour you usually open the app. We do not use your position, speed or messages for this. If you agreed to offer e-mails, we may send them at the hour you usually open the app; you can unsubscribe at any time. We also look for signs of misuse, such as many accounts on one phone or repeated failed payments. A person reviews these; nothing is blocked automatically. These counts are deleted with your account.

App diagnostics log

To find and fix problems such as a late alert or a dropped connection, the app keeps a technical log on your phone: app events and errors, alert and speech timing, audio focus and music volume changes, crash detection and SOS steps with their sensor numbers, GPS accuracy, connection drops, battery level and permission states. It is kept for at most 7 days and 20 MB, then deleted on the phone.

The log never contains message text, names, phone numbers, e-mail addresses, medical information, passwords or sign-in tokens, or addresses. Locations are rounded to about 100 metres and written only during a ride. Account ids are replaced by a short code that only your phone can make.

The log stays on your phone unless you choose to send it: from Profile, Help, Send feedback (turn on "Attach diagnostics log", or open View diagnostics and tap "Send logs to CoRoute" or "Share log file"), when you say yes to the question the app asks once after a ride with an SOS or crash alarm, or when our support team asks for it and you tap Send. "Send safety logs automatically" is off until you turn it on. Our server removes personal patterns again, keeps a sent log for 30 days for our support team only (every view and download is recorded), and deletes it at once when you delete your account. You can clear the log on your phone at any time.

Clubs and tour operators

Riding clubs and tour operators can run rides on CoRoute. When you join a club or sign up for one of their rides, the organiser and their co-organisers see your username and first name, and your live position only during their rides. They never see your phone number, e-mail or medical ID.

Your organiser sees your emergency contact only if you raise an SOS during their ride, only while that SOS is open, and every time they look at it is recorded. You can leave a club at any time.

Plans and payments

Group plans are bought on our website (Plans), not in the app. At times hosting group rides may be free for everyone, or free up to a limit; safety features are never part of a plan. Payments are handled by Razorpay. We receive the order, the amount, the payment id and whether it succeeded; your card, UPI or bank details go to Razorpay, not to us. We send your receipt by e-mail when e-mail is set up. Refunds follow our Refund and Cancellation Policy.

E-mails and notifications

These are the e-mails we send:

  • Welcome: once, when you create your account, with first steps and a link to the quick start guide. Its short "Group rides" part (how group rides work and a link to the group plans on our website) is left out if you turn off tips and news.

  • Ride summaries: after a ride of at least 2 km and 10 minutes, a summary of your own ride: date, distance, riding time, stops and how many riders were in the group, with a small picture of your route.   • Tips and news: occasional product news and riding tips. On until you turn it off.

At most a few a week, and not at night (India time).
  • Plan ending: if you bought a plan on our website, a note shortly before it ends (or after). It is about your account and has no offers or prices.

Links in our marketing e-mails may go through a short redirect on our site (/r/...) so we can count how many people clicked each link. Choose what you get under "E-mails from CoRoute" in your website account. Every e-mail you can turn off has an unsubscribe link at the bottom that works with one click, and the link turns off only that kind of e-mail. If e-mails to your address bounce or are reported as spam, we stop sending them.

The app can show push notifications through Google Firebase Cloud Messaging. You can turn off all of them in Settings, or each kind of news notification (news and updates, events, safety tips, community, partner perks; partner perks start off). Safety alerts, ride and friend notifications cannot be turned off separately, because they are part of how the app keeps your group safe.

Who can see your data

Members of the convoy you are in see your name, vehicle, phone, emergency contact, live position and messages. If you filled in medical info, they see it only while your SOS or crash alert is open. Medical info is never shown to administrators, never written into the ride timeline and never put in notifications. They can also see your recorded route, stops and timeline entries, but only for the time they were in that convoy themselves. Nobody outside your convoy can see them. Service administrators can see active convoys for safety operations (for example to dissolve an abusive convoy or send a safety announcement), open SOS alerts with who raised them, where and who is going to help, the safety log and false alarm counts; they cannot see medical info or listen to intercom audio. Riders of other groups see only what is described under "Help from nearby riders of other groups" and "Groups nearby". Club and tour operator organisers see only what is described under "Clubs and tour operators".

Ride Guardian viewers see only the individual rider who created the grant, limited strictly to the chosen tier (Basic: aggregate status without exact coordinates; Live: personal location during active ride only; Emergency: location only when SOS or crash alert is open). They cannot see convoy chat, rider rosters, contact numbers, or medical profiles. Sweeper assignments and rider-down incident reports are shared only with the active group, and approaching riders on the same road receive road safety warnings with no private personal data leaked.

Where your data lives

Data is stored in an Oracle Cloud Autonomous Database and relayed by our server, both in the Oracle Cloud Hyderabad region (India). Connections use TLS. Passwords are stored as scrypt hashes (older bcrypt hashes are replaced the next time you sign in). If you sign in with Google, we receive your e-mail and display name from Google; Google's own privacy policy governs that sign-in.
